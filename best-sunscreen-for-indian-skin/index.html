What makes a sunscreen work for Indian skin?

Three things: SPF 50 or higher, PA++++ (or broad-spectrum UVA protection), and a formulation that doesn't leave a white cast. Indian skin tones are usually in the Fitzpatrick III-VI range, so "no white cast" isn't a luxury - it is the difference between a product you wear daily and one that sits in the cupboard. Gel and gel-cream textures also survive Indian humidity better than thick creams.

One thing that matters more than brand: sunscreen only works if you apply it every day, in the right amount (about two finger-lengths for the face), and reapply if you are outside for hours. A ₹99 sunscreen you use daily beats a ₹1,400 one you skip.

How much sunscreen should you actually use?

  1. Two finger-lengths of sunscreen for the face and neck - most people use half of what they need.
  2. Apply it as the last skincare step before makeup, 15 minutes before sun exposure.
  3. Reapply every 2-3 hours if you are outdoors; every 4-5 hours for indoor office days near windows.
  4. Do not skip the ears, back of the neck, and the part-line in your hair.
  5. In Indian summers, gel textures reapply more comfortably than creams - worth factoring in.

Should you buy SPF 50 or SPF 70?

SPF 50 blocks about 98% of UVB; SPF 70 blocks about 98.3%. The difference is small, and the higher SPF often comes with a thicker or pricier formula. Foxtale's SPF 70 (₹400, 4.2 stars) is a reasonable choice if you spend hours outdoors, but for daily office use SPF 50 PA++++ is enough and easier to wear consistently.

FAQ

Which sunscreen is best for oily skin in India?

Deconstruct Gel SPF 50 PA++++ (₹224-321, 4.3 stars) is the top pick - gel base, matte finish, photostable filters. Foxtale SPF 70 (₹400) is the alternative if you want even higher protection.

Do Indian sunscreens leave a white cast?

Many older ones did, but current gel and gel-cream formulations (Deconstruct, Minimalist, Lacto Calamine) are designed to avoid white cast on Indian skin tones. Read recent reviews mentioning "no white cast" before buying any unfamiliar brand.

Is sunscreen needed indoors in India?

Yes, if you sit near windows - UVA passes through glass. An SPF 50 PA++++ product used daily indoors covers this with one morning application.

Which is better: SPF 50 or SPF 70?

For daily use, SPF 50 PA++++ is the sweet spot. SPF 70 (like Foxtale's) helps for extended outdoor exposure but the protection gain over SPF 50 is small.

Can sunscreen replace moisturiser in Indian summers?

For oily skin, often yes - many modern sunscreens (Minimalist, Deconstruct) contain humectants. Dry skin should still moisturise underneath.
