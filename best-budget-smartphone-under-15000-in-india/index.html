The short version

TL;DR: The best budget smartphones under Rs. 15,000 in India right now (prices checked 13 August 2026) are the Moto G37 5G (Rs. 14,800) for the best all-rounder, the Redmi A7 Pro 5G (Rs. 14,240) for the biggest screen and battery, and the Lava Bold N2 5G (Rs. 12,998) if you want to spend less. All three run Android 16, pack 5G and 120Hz displays, and are genuinely available under the budget after the recent Amazon Great Freedom Sale ended.

What really matters in a phone under Rs. 15,000 in 2026?

At this price you are giving something up, so it pays to know your priorities before you scroll. The three things that show up the most in real use are the chipset, the battery, and the storage. Avoid phones with barely 64GB of internal storage unless you rarely install apps, because 64GB fills fast with a few games and a year of photos.

Display and refresh rate matter more now than they did a couple of years ago, because a 120Hz panel is common even at Rs. 13,000. Also check charging speed: a big battery is only useful if it charges in reasonable time. And look at the software-update promise, not just the version number - a phone that launches on Android 16 is fine, but you want to know if it will actually get future updates.

Is the Moto G37 5G the best all-rounder under Rs. 15,000?

Yes, for most people. The Moto G37 5G launched in April 2026 and sits at Rs. 14,800 on the day I checked. It runs the MediaTek Dimensity 6300 chipset, which handles daily apps and light gaming without much trouble, and it is the only phone in this list to ship with 256GB of storage as standard. That is a genuinely useful amount at this price - you will not be deleting apps to make room.

The phone also has Gorilla Glass 7i, an IP64 rating, an in-display fingerprint reader, and a 50-megapixel main camera. Two things stand out for the money: it supports wireless charging alongside 20W wired fast charging, which you almost never see under Rs. 15,000, and it runs on Hello UI based on Android 16.

When to pick: the Moto G37 5G if you want the most balanced phone in the budget - good screen, big storage, near-stock Android, and that rare wireless charging.
Avoid if: you need a larger battery or play demanding games. The 5,200mAh cell is fine but not class-leading, and the Dimensity 6300 is competent rather than a gaming chip.

Is the Redmi A7 Pro 5G the best value for a big screen and long battery?

The Redmi A7 Pro 5G is the bargain flagship of this group. With a 6.9-inch 120Hz display and a 6,300mAh battery at Rs. 14,240, it gives you the largest screen and the largest battery in this list. It launched in April 2026 on HyperOS 3 based on Android 16, and the base 4GB/64GB model can be expanded via microSD up to 2TB, so storage is not a hard limit.

The trade-off is the camera: a 32-megapixel rear shooter with an f/2.0 aperture is workable in good light but noticeably weaker in low light than the 50-megapixel options on the Moto and on many rivals. Charging is also slow at 15W, which is on the low side for a 6,300mAh battery; a full charge takes a while.

When to pick: the Redmi A7 Pro 5G if screen size and battery life top your list and you mostly shoot photos in daylight.
Avoid if: low-light cameras or fast charging matter to you, or if you want more than 4GB of RAM in the base model.

Should you spend less and get the Lava Bold N2 5G?

At Rs. 12,998 the Lava Bold N2 5G is the cheapest phone here. It launched in June 2026 with a Unisoc T8200 chipset, 4GB RAM, 64GB storage, a 6.75-inch 120Hz display, and a 6,000mAh battery - and it is an Indian brand, which matters to some buyers. You pay for the price with modest specs: a 13-megapixel rear camera and tight 64GB storage. For basic calls, messaging, browsing, and social media it is more than enough.

When to pick: the Lava Bold N2 5G if you want to stay close to Rs. 13,000 and use your phone for everyday essentials rather than gaming or photography.
Avoid if: camera quality or storage capacity are important, or if you plan to use the phone as your primary device for a few years.

Is the Samsung Galaxy M17 5G an option under Rs. 15,000?

The Galaxy M17 5G is the awkward one in this list, and I want to be straight about why. It launched in October 2025 at Rs. 12,499 for the 4GB/128GB model, and Samsung positioned it squarely in the sub-Rs. 15,000 segment. It has a bright AMOLED display and NFC for payments, both rare at this price. But on the day I checked (13 August 2026), the market price on Flipkart had climbed well above Rs. 15,000 - around Rs. 16,785 for the base model.

So the Galaxy M17 5G is only a real under-Rs. 15,000 option during a sale, when retailers drop it back toward its launch price. If you are in no hurry and want an AMOLED panel and NFC under budget, it is worth watching for the next sale. The Moto G37 remains the safer buy if you want a verified sub-Rs. 15,000 phone today.

When to pick: the Galaxy M17 5G during a sale that brings the 4GB/128GB model back under Rs. 15,000 - you get AMOLED and NFC that nothing else here matches.
Avoid if: you need a phone now. Its typical daily price sits above this budget, so it does not belong in a strict under-Rs. 15,000 roundup unless discounted.

How do you pick the right one for your use case?

  1. Decide your budget number first - under Rs. 13,000 points to the Lava Bold N2, around Rs. 14,000-14,800 opens up the Moto and Redmi.
  2. Prioritise two specs max. Big screen and battery: Redmi A7 Pro. Storage and balanced everything: Moto G37. Lowest price: Lava Bold N2.
  3. Check the RAM and storage variant you are actually buying, not just the headline price - 4GB/128GB beats 6GB/64GB for daily use.
  4. Watch for sale pricing on the Galaxy M17 if you want AMOLED and NFC and are willing to wait.
  5. Verify the final price on the listing before checkout, because prices here swing with sales and even across the day.

FAQ

Which is the best phone under Rs. 15,000 in India in 2026?

For most buyers the Moto G37 5G is the best all-rounder: 256GB storage, a 50-megapixel camera, wireless charging, and near-stock Android 16 at around Rs. 14,800. The Redmi A7 Pro 5G wins on screen size and battery, and the Lava Bold N2 5G is the cheapest at about Rs. 13,000.

Will I get 5G on a budget phone under Rs. 15,000?

Yes. All three phones covered here - the Moto G37 5G, Redmi A7 Pro 5G, and Lava Bold N2 5G - support 5G. Budget 5G support has become standard in this segment since 2025, at least as a network band, though actual speeds still depend on your carrier coverage.

How much RAM and storage do I need in a phone under Rs. 15,000?

Prefer 4GB of RAM with 128GB of storage over 6GB RAM with 64GB storage. Storage fills up faster than you expect, and 64GB disappears quickly. The Moto G37's 256GB is a real advantage; the Lava Bold N2's 64GB is workable only if you keep apps light.

Is it better to buy a Chinese brand or an Indian brand at this price?

Value and software support usually matter more than brand origin. The Moto and Redmi offer better specs and cameras per rupee; Lava is Indian-made and fine for basic use. Buy based on your priorities rather than on origin alone, and check the promised OS updates for whichever phone you choose.
